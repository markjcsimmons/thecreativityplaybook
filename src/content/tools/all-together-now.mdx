## How to play

1. **Agree on a challenge.** Four or more of you, one creative problem.
2. **Write for two minutes.** Everyone writes ideas on their own sheet of paper, leaving room underneath.
3. **Pass to the left.** When the timer ends, everyone passes their sheet to the player on their left. On the sheet you receive, write new ideas triggered by the ones already there.
4. **Keep passing.** Do this every two minutes, until each sheet is back with the player who started it.
5. **Read out and vote.** Take turns reading out your sheet. Each player gives 3 points to their favorite idea, 2 to their second favorite and 1 to their third.
6. **Count the votes.** The player whose idea got the most points wins.

## How this page works

The book's game is played on paper, because passing the sheets is the point. This page is the facilitator. It runs the two-minute timer for each pass, tells you when to pass and counts the votes. Everyone needs a sheet of paper and a pen, and you can play on one shared screen or in a call.

## Why it works

The Idea Commune chapter is about working with other people to come up with ideas, and ideas get bigger when people build on each other. The book compares the game to Chinese Whispers, where a phrase changes as it passes from person to person until it bears no resemblance to where it started. Here the change is the point. Each person who reads a sheet takes the ideas somewhere new, and by the time the sheet is back with its owner it has gone far past anything one person would have written.

The book's last line on it is that love is more important than winning. The vote is there to find the best idea, not to produce a loser.

## Questions

**How many people do we need?** Four or more. The book says the more the merrier, and the timer here supports up to 12.

**How many passes are there?** One for each player, so every sheet is passed all the way round.

**Can I vote for my own idea?** The book does not say. We suggest voting for other people's ideas, but agree that in your group.
