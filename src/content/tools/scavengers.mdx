## How to play

1. **Start on point 1.** The dial has ten points, numbered clockwise.
2. **Answer a question.** There are twelve, and each has two possible answers. One is right and one is wrong.
3. **Move your marker.** Each answer tells you how many spaces to go and in which direction. Your position has nothing to do with which question you are on.
4. **Dig.** After twelve answers you are on one of the ten points. Each point leads to a different X on the treasure map, and only one has the treasure.

## Why it works

The Scavengers chapter says there is a treasure trove of ideas lying around, and the trick is to keep your wits about you. If you look carefully, you notice things that other people overlook or take for granted. This game tests exactly that, with questions about things you have seen thousands of times: a traffic light, a paper clip, a phone keypad.

A wrong answer sends you to the wrong place, so one careless guess can cost you the treasure.

## Look things up

The questions are about the real world, so you can go and look. Check a traffic light, count the sides of a pencil, find a phone. That fits the chapter's advice to keep your eyes open.

## Where this comes from

This is the Scavenger Game from the Scavengers chapter, played on screen. The book's board has a treasure map with ten Xs, and you find the grid reference of the right one. Here you get the same dial and the same twelve questions, and the game tells you where the treasure is at the end. The questions, the moves, the answers and the treasure reference are the book's. The picture for question 12 is cropped from the book. Nothing you type leaves your browser and nothing is stored.

## Questions

**Do I get told if an answer is wrong as I go?** No. You find out at the end, as in the book.

**Why does the worked example give away two answers?** It follows the book's own answer page, which walks through the first answers. The other ten are yours to find.

**Is this a way to practice noticing ideas?** It is the first step. The chapter goes on to techniques such as listing what bugs you about an everyday product. Try the [Attribute Listing Tool](/tools/attribute-listing/) next.
