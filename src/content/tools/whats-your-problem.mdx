## How to play

1. **Write your problem.** Start it with "How can we ...", for example "How can we sell more music?"
2. **Roll the die.** It gives you a kind of question: 1 is HOW, 2 is WHY, 3 is WHICH, 4 is WHEN, 5 is WHO and 6 is WHAT.
3. **Ask that question about your problem and answer it.** Write your own question if you like, then your answer. There are eight steps.
4. **Rewrite the problem.** At the last space, use what you found to write the problem again as "How can we ...?" The book says to keep it simple and clear.

## Why it works

The book's point in this chapter is that better questions get better answers. Its example is a band. If the band asks "How do we sell more of our music?", it has already assumed that selling music is the best way to make money from music. If it asks "How do we make money from our music?", it has not. The two questions lead to very different answers.

The game makes you question the first version of your problem from six angles before you try to solve it. The rewrite at the end is the real result, and it is often more specific and easier to act on than the problem you started with.

## What the six questions are for

The die only gives you the kind of question. What you ask is up to you. In the book's example, the band's WHAT question was about the barriers to selling music, its WHY question was about why people spend less, and its WHEN question was about when people do spend money. A HOW roll can be used to rephrase the problem, and a WHO roll asks who is involved, such as who pays for extra content when the music is free elsewhere.

## Where this comes from

This is the What's Your Problem? board game at the end of the Answer Is in the Question chapter, played on screen. The rules and the example are the book's. The board has eight question spaces, and so does this version. Nothing you type leaves your browser and nothing is stored.

## Questions

**What if I roll the same word twice?** That is allowed. Ask a different question each time.

**What if my answer shows I picked the wrong problem?** Good. That is what the game is for. You can play again with the new problem.

**Is there a right answer?** No. The book says different answers could have led the band to a different rewrite, such as using the recordings to promote live gigs instead of trying to sell them.
