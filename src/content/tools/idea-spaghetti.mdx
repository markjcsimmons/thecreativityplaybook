## How to play

1. **Pick a fork.** The game deals you a topic and a target. The target is how many ideas you need in five minutes.
2. **Start the five minutes.** Type every idea you can think of and press Enter after each one. Quantity matters more than quality.
3. **Score the round.** You get a point for every idea over the target and lose a point for every idea under it. Beat a target of 12 with 15 ideas and you score 3. Come up with only 10 and you lose 2.
4. **Play four rounds.** Each round has a different topic and a different target. Your final score is the total.

You can swap the dealt topic for a problem of your own in any round. The book suggests starting with its topics as a warm-up and moving to your own problems once you have got the hang of generating lots of ideas fast.

## Why it works

The Idea Spaghetti chapter argues that the more ideas you come up with, the more likely it is that you will have a good one. The game is not about solving a problem. The book describes it as a brain-train: it practices generating lots of ideas, or spaghetti, rather than judging them. The target and the clock keep you from stopping to decide whether an idea is any good.

## The targets and topics

The targets are the numbers on the ingredients on the book's board. On the board you follow a strand from your fork to an ingredient, and here a target is dealt at random. The topics are the book's list, such as "new names for pasta", "uses for a brick" and "ways to generate power". We left out one topic that does not suit a workplace site.

## Where this comes from

This is the Idea Spaghetti board game from the Idea Spaghetti chapter, played on screen. The rules, the topics and the scoring are the book's. The [Random Idea Generator](/tools/random-idea-generator/) also has a Spaghetti mode, with a fixed target of 50 that is our own. Nothing you type leaves your browser and nothing is stored.

## Questions

**Do repeats count?** Only exact repeats are dropped. Whether two ideas are really different is up to you.

**Can the score go negative?** Yes. Fall short of a target and you lose points, as in the book.

**Can I play with other people?** The book's game is for one to four players, each with a fork. Here you play alone. To play with others, deal yourselves the same topic and compare scores.
