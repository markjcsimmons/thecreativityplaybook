## The big idea

Being in control feels reassuring in business, and chaos goes against the grain. Once in a while, the chapter says, you need a little orchestrated chaos to get the creative engines revving.

## What's in the chapter

Great writers and musicians have used randomness to make great work. Dave describes setting up equipment in unusual places, instead of an expensive studio, once he and Annie Lennox had their first hits, because the odd locations triggered different moods and kept the records sounding unusual. The chapter also shows how arbitrary words can throw something random into the mix.

Humor is another way in. It introduces the unexpected and changes the direction of thinking that may be going stale, and the book says it has a direct relationship to creative ability.

At bottom, creativity is connecting two seemingly unconnected, often opposing pieces. As Paul Allen and Evan Williams show, the resulting idea often looks obvious in hindsight.
