## The big idea

When you free your mind from linear thinking, you can explore ideas a straight line would never reach. The opening chapter argues that creativity is not a talent for a few artists. Everyone starts with it, and play is how it comes out.

## What's in the chapter

The chapter starts with music, which the authors treat as creativity at its purest: one original idea can break through and reach millions of people. Dave describes his stepfather, Julien, who had amazing ideas every day and put them to use, as an example of creativity in ordinary life.

Then it turns to what happens to us. As children we had it. As we get older it is undervalued or sidelined, and we learn rules, go to school, get a job and conform. The authors cite data showing a significant drop in our ability to think divergently as we grow up, and strong evidence that the ability is still there. Evan Williams, a co-founder of Twitter, told the authors for the 2010 edition that he thinks people can learn to be creative but, for the most part, unlearn how to do it.

The chapter ends with business. Creativity needs encouraging so that it becomes part of what a company does, even though it can feel a little frightening and new ideas meet real barriers. Companies that want to thrive, the authors say, need to let it come out to play again.
