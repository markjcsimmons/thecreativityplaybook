## The big idea

Quantity first. You cannot plan your way to the one great idea, so the sensible move is to produce a ton of ideas and let the good ones turn up among them.

## What's in the chapter

To do that you have to think divergently, which means opening up options instead of narrowing to a single answer.

Brainstorms are one way in, but the chapter calls them a mixed blessing: done badly, they can be counterproductive. A good one has clear parameters, a mix of people from different backgrounds, a bit of healthy competition and high expectations for performance.

The chapter's last point is that creativity is not about finding the one idea that will change the world. It is a journey, which the authors call the innovation train that never really ends.
