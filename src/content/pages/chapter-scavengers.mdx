## The big idea

Always be on the hunt for things to improve. Keep your eyes, ears and nose open to what is out there, the chapter says, and your mind gets tuned to new possibilities.

## What's in the chapter

However much you love the way something is done, there is always something to improve. The chapter points to Patagonia, which, as the 2010 edition describes it, set itself high goals and was open about what it could still do better to reduce its environmental impact.

Change is guaranteed, even if it is hard to see which disruptive change is waiting around the corner. The book's example is the music business, where the revenue model changed dramatically and the smart bands embraced the change and turned it to their advantage.

The foundation of innovation, the chapter says, is natural curiosity and a habit of constantly taking in information and ideas, as Microsoft co-founder Paul Allen has shown across his many ventures. The chapter also introduces attribute listing, a way of finding improvements by listing the attributes of a thing and asking what could be better about each.
