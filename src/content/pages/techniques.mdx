## How this list works

Most lists of ideation techniques are lists of famous methods. This one is a list of what the book teaches. Each entry says what the technique does, names the chapter and links to a page that explains it or lets you try it. Methods the book does not teach are not in this list. A few we have written up anyway sit in a separate section near the end, each marked as outside the book.

The techniques are grouped by the job they do: making ideas, reframing a problem, finding ideas that are lying around, getting unstuck, and choosing and launching. The book keeps making ideas and judging them separate, so start with a technique from the first four groups and finish with one from the last.

## Making ideas

### Idea spaghetti (Chapter 2)

Make as many ideas as you can in a short time against a target, without judging. The book's argument is that the more ideas you make, the more likely one is good. [Play Idea Spaghetti](/games/idea-spaghetti/) or print the [brainstorming template](/templates/brainstorming-template/).

### Brainwriting (Chapter 7)

Everyone writes ideas on paper, passes the sheet round and builds on what they receive. The book calls it All Together Now and does not use the word brainwriting. [Run it with the timer](/games/all-together-now/).

### Building with blocks (Chapter 7)

Add one idea to a second to make a third, then keep stacking. The book also calls it successive element integration. The steps are on the [brainstorming games](/exercises/brainstorming-games/) page.

### Forcing a connection (Chapter 10)

Connect two things that seem unrelated. Two ways to do it:

- **A random word.** Pick a word from a dictionary and force a link to your problem.<IfLive path="/techniques/random-word-technique/"> See the [random word technique](/techniques/random-word-technique/).</IfLive>
- **Two random themes.** [Inspiral](/games/inspiral/) deals two themes and gives you three minutes to link them.

### Pictures and similes (Chapter 6)

Compare your problem to a picture: "It is like ... because ...". The [Brain Flash game](/games/brain-flash/) deals the pictures. The book's collage version splits a problem among four to eight people who each sketch a piece.<IfLive path="/techniques/analogical-thinking/"> The [analogical thinking](/techniques/analogical-thinking/) page covers the simile and metaphor methods.</IfLive><IfLive path="/guides/visualization-techniques/"> The [visualization techniques](/guides/visualization-techniques/) page covers the chapter's before-and-after sketch, the photo method and the collage.</IfLive>

### The banana skin (Chapter 10)

A joke or provocative image dropped into stale thinking, to break the rut. The [creative block](/guides/creative-block/) guide uses it.

## Reframing a problem

### Question your assumptions (Chapter 4)

List what you are taking for granted, and test each by flipping it to its opposite. See [creative problem solving](/guides/creative-problem-solving/).

### Reword the problem (Chapter 4)

Write it as "How can we ...?" and reword it several ways. The [problem statement examples](/guides/problem-statement-examples/) guide has a method and a template. The [What's Your Problem? game](/games/whats-your-problem/) uses six question words, which [asking better questions](/guides/asking-better-questions/) explains.

### Split the problem (Chapter 4)

Break a big problem into smaller ones with the cherry (a tree of smaller problems) or the fishbone diagram. See [creative problem solving](/guides/creative-problem-solving/).

### Other people's shoes (Chapters 4 and 9)

Look at the problem as a mayor, a police chief, an environmentalist or a commuter would, or as whoever the dice give you. Play [Shoe Swapper](/games/shoe-swapper/).

### Exaggerate and dramatize (Chapter 8)

Push the problem to an extreme to pull your thinking away from the obvious. Play the [paperclip game](/games/far-out/) and read [storytelling in business](/guides/storytelling-in-business/).

## Finding ideas that are lying around

### The bug list (Chapter 3)

List what annoys you about a product or service. Each bug is a possible improvement. See [invention ideas](/guides/invention-ideas/).

### Attribute listing (Chapter 3)

Break a product into its attributes, list the options for each and recombine. Use the [Attribute Listing Tool](/tools/attribute-listing/).

### Notebooks and idea jams (Chapter 12)

Write ideas down as they come, and share them in a regular gathering. See [innovation culture](/guides/innovation-culture/) and the [Idea Notebook](/notebook/).

## Getting unstuck

### Walk away on purpose (Chapter 5)

Rest the problem with something absorbing and unrelated, so unconscious thought can work. Spin the [Wheel of Distraction](/games/wheel-of-distraction/).<IfLive path="/guides/whole-brain-thinking/"> The [whole brain thinking](/guides/whole-brain-thinking/) guide explains the research.</IfLive>

## Choosing and launching

### Divergent, then convergent (Chapter 2)

Make many options first, then narrow. Read [divergent vs convergent thinking](/techniques/divergent-vs-convergent-thinking/).

### Kill the idea (Chapter 11)

A random idea is about to be killed, and you have three saves. It shows which idea you would fight for. [Play Kill the Idea](/games/kill-the-idea/).

### Name it, write the news story, find the crew (Chapter 12)

Turn one idea into something tangible. [Play Blast Off](/games/blast-off/) and use the [product launch plan template](/templates/product-launch-plan-template/).

### Pitch it (Chapter 1)

Say it in 30 seconds to someone who can say yes. See the [elevator pitch examples](/guides/elevator-pitch-examples/).

## From outside the book

These methods are not in the book. Each page says so and shows where the book's own habits are similar.

- **SCAMPER.** Seven questions to ask of something that exists. See [SCAMPER](/techniques/scamper/).
<IfLive path="/techniques/reverse-brainstorming/">
- **Reverse brainstorming.** Ask how to make a goal fail, then flip each answer into a fix. See [reverse brainstorming](/techniques/reverse-brainstorming/).
</IfLive>
<IfLive path="/techniques/six-thinking-hats/">
- **The six thinking hats.** Six kinds of thinking, taken one at a time, so that ideas and criticism do not arrive together. See the [six thinking hats](/techniques/six-thinking-hats/).
</IfLive>
<IfLive path="/techniques/five-whys/">
- **The five whys.** Ask "why?" until you reach a cause you can fix. See the [five whys](/techniques/five-whys/).
</IfLive>
<IfLive path="/guides/first-principles-thinking/">

</IfLive>
<IfLive path="/guides/mind-mapping/">

## Which technique when

This guide is ours. The book does not match techniques to situations.

- **You have a problem and no ideas.** Reword the problem, then make a pile of ideas with Idea Spaghetti.
- **You have the same ideas as everybody.** Force a connection with a random word or Inspiral, or put yourself in other people's shoes.
- **You are stuck.** Walk away, or throw in a banana skin.
- **You have too many ideas.** Kill the Idea, then Blast Off for the survivor.
- **You are working with a team.** Brainwriting and building with blocks. See [brainstorming games](/exercises/brainstorming-games/) and the [ideation workshop](/exercises/team-workshop-activities/) agenda.
- **You want a quick warm-up.** The [creative thinking exercises](/exercises/creative-thinking-exercises/) page has ten you can run in minutes.

## Questions

**What is the best ideation technique?** The book does not rank them. It says that making many ideas before judging is the habit behind most of them, so Idea Spaghetti is a good first one.

**Are these the same as brainstorming methods?** Several are. Brainwriting, building with blocks and Idea Spaghetti are brainstorming methods. Others work on the problem before you brainstorm or on the ideas afterward.

**Does the book teach SCAMPER or the six thinking hats?** No. This page covers the methods the book teaches.

**Which techniques work alone?** Most of them. The table of group methods is on the [brainstorming games](/exercises/brainstorming-games/) page.
