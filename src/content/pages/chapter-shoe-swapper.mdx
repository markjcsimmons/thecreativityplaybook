## The big idea

Look at the problem through someone else's eyes. What we already know, or think we know, about what will work can hold us back.

## What's in the chapter

The chapter describes a research study in which people tapped out familiar tunes with their knuckles and tried to get others to recognize them. It shows how what you already know skews your perceptions and your judgment.

Looking through the eyes of someone else, such as a child or a passenger in a cab, gives you fresh perspectives that are not hemmed in by old, rigid thinking.

In business, that means asking for views from, or trying on the shoes of, people who are not experts: colleagues from other departments, or people from completely unrelated professions. The Shoe Swapper game on this site uses roles and archetypes rather than named people.
