## The big idea

The answer is often in the question. How you frame a problem sends your thinking in a particular direction, so it is worth working on the problem before you hunt for solutions.

## What's in the chapter

Start with some navel-gazing. List the assumptions you are making about the situation, then throw out the ones that do not hold true. The 2010 edition's example is Nokia, which questioned basic assumptions about its mix of businesses before moving into mobile phones. That is a 2010 snapshot, so read it as a story about questioning assumptions, not as a picture of the company today.

Next, frame the problem more than one way. The chapter reworks traffic congestion into different versions of the problem, and each version sends your creativity somewhere new.

When a problem just seems too big, break it into smaller pieces. The book notes that the technique has been used successfully in shipyards, car design and stage productions.
