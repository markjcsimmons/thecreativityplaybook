A lateral thinking puzzle describes a strange situation and asks you to explain it, or to solve a problem in an unexpected way. They are hard when you follow the obvious reading and easy when you drop an assumption you did not know you had. If you want the idea behind them, start with [lateral thinking](/lateral-thinking/).

## How to get the most from these puzzles

1. **Say your first guess out loud, then doubt it.** Your first reading is where the hidden assumption lives.
2. **Ask what the puzzle is not telling you.** The missing detail is usually the clue.
3. **Use the hints one at a time.** They are ordered from a nudge to a bigger push. Notice which one cracks it.
4. **Read the thinking move.** It names the habit the puzzle trains, so you can use it on real problems.

## Questions

**Are these the classic lateral thinking puzzles?** No. We wrote them for this site, in our own words. A few use familiar ideas, such as a rule you have to guess, but none is copied from a puzzle book or another site.

**Is there only one answer?** Some puzzles have one intended answer. The "open challenge" style ones, where the question is how you would fix something, say "one good answer", and others can work. If you found a better one, you did the job.

**How is the puzzle of the day chosen?** The page picks one from the list each calendar day, in your browser. There is no account and nothing is stored.

**Can I share a puzzle?** Yes. Each puzzle has an address you can link to, for example `/lateral-thinking/puzzles/#p-dry-swimmer`. Please link to the page instead of copying the list.
