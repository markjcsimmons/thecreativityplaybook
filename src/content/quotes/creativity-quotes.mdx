## How this list works

The first quotes on this page are from the interviews the authors did for *The Business Playground* (2010), the earlier edition of the book. We checked each against the printed text, and they keep their original British spelling. The authors' own lines, from the same book and from the draft introduction to the new edition, are marked as theirs.

A few well-known lines are here too. For those we traced each quote to where it was first published, and the credit under it says where. A quote with no source we could find is left out, which is why this list is shorter than most.

## About the outside quotes

- **Einstein, "not to stop questioning."** Quote Investigator traces it to an interview reported in *LIFE* on 2 May 1955, shortly after Einstein's death, and finds substantive evidence he said it. Later books changed a word, so we use the 1955 wording.
- **Einstein, "Imagination is more important than knowledge."** It comes from a 1929 interview in *The Saturday Evening Post*. Quote Investigator concludes the quotation is accurate. A similar line often shared as Einstein's ("Imagination is everything...") has no evidence behind it, so it is not here.
- **Pauling, "a lot of ideas."** A shorter version, "the best way to have a good idea is to have lots of ideas," is repeated more often. We use the fuller wording, from a 1969 newspaper report, which is the earliest full public version Quote Investigator found.
- **Edison, "one per cent inspiration."** An earlier 1901 newspaper gives Edison the same line, and other versions use two per cent and ninety-eight. We use the 1932 *Harper's* account, in which Edison is quoted repeating it.

## Use a quote as a prompt

This part is ours, not the book's. A quote works best as a question to answer, not a poster to look at.

1. Pick the quote that annoys you or surprises you most.
2. Write down what it would mean in your own work this week, in one sentence.
3. Try it once. If it was about being stuck, stay stuck for ten minutes before you reach for help. If it was about having many ideas, write twenty.

## Questions

**Where do the quotes come from?** Most come from interviews the authors did for the 2010 edition of the book. The credit under each one gives the chapter. The four from outside the book name the publication and date.

**Can I share them?** Short quotes with the credit are fine to share. The Copy quote button puts the quote and its credit on your clipboard so the name stays attached.

**Why are there so few compared with other lists?** We only include quotes we could trace to a source or to a chapter of the book. Many quotes on other lists are given a famous name and no source.

**Why do some quotes use British spelling?** They are printed as the book printed them. We keep a quote's original wording, even where the rest of the site uses US spelling.

**Are these quotes in the new edition?** The 2010 edition's interviews may not all appear in the new edition, whose layout is not final. The lines marked as from the new edition come from a draft.
