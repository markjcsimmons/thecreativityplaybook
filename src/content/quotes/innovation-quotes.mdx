## How this list works

Most of these quotes come from the interviews the authors did for *The Business Playground* (2010), the earlier edition of the book. We checked each against the printed text, and they keep their original British spelling. Roles are as the book gave them at the time, so an executive from 2010 is listed as "then" in that job.

Two lines come from the book's Introduction and are not from the authors' own interviews. One is Jack Welch's advice, which the book quotes from a 1998 biography. The other is Tim Brown's, printed without an interview credit. The Edison and Schumpeter lines are from outside the book, each traced to where it was printed.

## The Edison line

Quote Investigator finds the "one per cent inspiration" saying in a 1901 newspaper and finds other versions that use two per cent and ninety-eight. We use the 1932 *Harper's* account, in which Edison is quoted repeating the line. It is a line about the effort after the idea, which is why it sits under "Getting an idea into the world."

## The Schumpeter line

The sentence is from the chapter on creative destruction in *Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy*, which was first published in 1942. Three sources we checked give the wording above and place it on page 83 of the third edition (Harper & Brothers, 1950), so that is the edition we cite. We did not check the 1942 text. The line is about how new things replace old ones, which is why it sits under "Staying ahead of change." It is an economist's view of whole industries, and the book's lines in this group are about single companies.

## Use a quote in a team meeting

This part is ours, not the book's.

1. Print one quote and put it where the team can see it.
2. Ask: "Where are we doing the same thing a little bit better, and what would the non-linear version look like?" (This borrows from the Ojanperä quote.)
3. Give everyone two minutes to write an answer alone, then read them out. The book's point, from the Chapter 2 quote above, is to reward the group as a whole for the best idea, so people build on each other's ideas and do not fight to make sure theirs wins.

## Questions

**Where do the quotes come from?** Most come from the interviews the authors did for the 2010 edition of the book. The credit under each gives the chapter, and the Edison line names the publication.

**Why are the roles marked "then"?** Because the book printed them in 2010. People change jobs, and we do not want to give anyone a title they no longer hold.

**Can I share them?** Short quotes with the credit are fine to share. The Copy quote button puts the quote and its credit on your clipboard.

**Why so few compared with other lists?** We only include quotes we can trace to a source or a chapter. Many quotes on other lists have a famous name attached and no source.
