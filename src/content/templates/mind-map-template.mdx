## What a mind map is

A mind map has a topic in the middle and branches that spread out from it. Each branch is one theme, and smaller branches hang from it with the details. Because everything starts from one point, you can see at a glance which parts of a topic are full and which are empty.

**The method is not from the book.** Most sources credit the British author Tony Buzan with popularizing the term, through a 1970s BBC series called *Use Your Head*. Drawings that branch out from a center are older than the name, and sources disagree about how much he invented, so treat that as the usual account.

**The book does show mind maps.** In Chapter 6, Dave Stewart shares one from a series made at a session hosted by the business strategist John Kao and the mind-map artist Lesley Evans, working on five-foot pieces of paper. The book says it is one of about ten that came out of that session. Chapter 2 has another, for the vision behind one of Dave's own ideas. The book shows them as pictures and gives no rules, so the steps below are ours.

## Make one here

Type a topic in the center. Then write your branches one per line, and indent a line by two spaces to hang it under the line above. The map draws as you type, and you can download it, print it or save the ideas to your notebook.

## How to fill it in

These steps are ours.

1. **Write the topic in the center, as a few words.** "Traffic in Los Angeles" works better than a whole sentence.
2. **Choose five to eight main branches.** Each is one part of the topic. If you are stuck, start with the six question words: who, what, why, when, how and which. They are the six the book's What's Your Problem? game rolls a die to choose from in Chapter 4, and the tool has a button that adds them as branches.
3. **Use one or two words per branch.** Short labels leave room to think. A sentence tends to close a branch instead of opening it.
4. **Hang ideas from each branch.** Keep going until a branch runs dry. Go back to an empty branch and ask why it is empty.
5. **Look for links across the map.** An idea on one branch often belongs on another. Those crossings are where new ideas come from.
6. **Pick the branches to work on.** A map is a picture of what you know and do not know. It is not a plan. Choose, and then sort the ideas with the book's three piles or the Idea Scorer.

## Mind map or list?

A mind map is not always better than a list. This is our own view.

- **Use a map when** you are exploring a topic, you do not know yet how its parts fit, or a group is working at one board.
- **Use a list when** you already know the order, for example the steps of a plan.
- **Use the book's games when** you want a quick start. A game gives you a prompt, a target and a clock, and a blank map gives you none of them.

## Common mistakes

- **Writing sentences.** Long labels turn a map into a page of text.
- **One branch only.** A map with one long chain is a list in disguise. Add branches from the center.
- **Stopping at the first level.** The ideas live on the smaller branches.
- **Judging while you draw.** Make the map first and choose afterwards. The book keeps the two apart, and so should you.
- **Mapping what you already know.** A map of the answers you have is a record. Add a branch for what you do not know.

## Questions

**What is a mind map template?** A ready-made layout for a mind map: a topic in the middle with branches around it. This page has a maker that draws one from a list and a blank sheet to print.

**Is the mind map in the book?** The book shows mind maps in Chapter 6 and Chapter 2 but does not teach a method for them. The steps on this page are ours.

**Who invented the mind map?** Most sources credit Tony Buzan with popularizing the term in the 1970s. Branching diagrams are older, and sources differ on how much he invented.

**Can I print it?** Yes. Print the blank sheet from the button, or draw a map in the maker and press Print. In the print dialog you can choose "Save as PDF".

**Can I save the map as a file?** Yes. Download the map as an SVG, which opens in a browser and in most drawing programs. You can also copy it or save it as an indented text outline.

**Is what I type kept or sent anywhere?** No. It stays in your browser, and nothing is kept unless you press Save the ideas to notebook.

**How many branches should I use?** Five to eight main branches is a workable range. The tool draws up to 100 lines in three levels.
